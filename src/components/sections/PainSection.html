Pain Points

What blocks enterprise AI was never the model.

Nearly every company has run an AI pilot in the last two years; very few made it into production. We hit the same walls in our own warehouses, transportation and customer service—the problems sit in four places: knowledge, semantics, execution and reuse.

Knowledge is locked in people’s heads and document piles

SOPs live in slide decks, chat groups and veterans’ experience. Feed a large model enough material and it only looks like it has read it—answers can’t be traced to a source, and nobody dares sign off on production.

70%of business knowledge is never structured

General models don’t know the industry’s tacit knowledge

“Dock”, “wave”, “LFD” and “hold” mean different things to different customers. Without a business ontology an Agent can’t be accurate, consistent or explainable in an enterprise setting.

0general models understand your SOPs out of the box

A chasm between POC and production

A smooth demo conversation meets permissions, approvals, idempotency, retries after disconnects, audit and cost metering in production. Without execution infrastructure, Agents stay toys.

< 10%of AI pilots reach production systems

One-off delivery that can’t be reused or metered

Every project writes prompts from scratch, integrates systems from scratch and builds governance from scratch. Capabilities have no versions, no evaluation, no distribution—ten projects cost ten times.

10×repeated implementation cost
Our view

The long-term assets of enterprise AI are the company’s own knowledge, ontologies, business skills, run records and improvement experience; the underlying model is a replaceable execution resource. The real moat is turning one-off implementation into capabilities that are reusable, configurable, distributable and meterable.
